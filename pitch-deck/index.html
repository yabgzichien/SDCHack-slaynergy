The gap: today’s career tools give advice with no dates, no sources and no track record.

Meet a 27-year-old bank clerk, laid off in November, with three to six months of allowance. In Malaysia, clerical roles were almost ten percent of online job ads in 2022. They are 7.3 percent now. And Anthropic’s Economic Index finds 43 percent of a general office clerk’s tasks already show up in real AI use. Career tools tell her what to do, but with no dates, no sources, and no track record. That is the gap.

Two users: the worker and the advisor.

The worker

A finance or admin clerk, 22 to 35, in the first week after being laid off.

  • 3 to 6 months of allowance: a real deadline
  • Needs to know which skills expire, which jobs are within reach, and what to learn this week

The advisor

An employment counsellor who meets each worker once.

  • Sees the worker’s profile, plan and the futures behind the advice
  • Writes advice that lands on the worker’s plan

Forecasts graded against real data.

  • Settled by official releases. Each question closes on a public release: DOSM on 17 Nov 2026, Epoch AI in Jan 2027.
  • Forecasters with a record. About 200 simulated forecasters built track records by replaying 66 real past releases. Always labelled simulated.
  • A hash log, not a blockchain. Every guess and settlement is chained, and the fingerprint is committed to GitHub.
This is not a chatbot. No AI model runs inside Jobbery. Scores and plans come from tested code: same inputs, same answer.

The hardest risks, and the fallback for each.

Official data comes out rarelyDOSM publishes once a quarter and Epoch AI about as often, so a live market could sit idle.
Replay real history66 past releases, 2021 Q1 to 2026 Q1, give every forecaster a track record on day one.
Only a few real forecasters exist so farA market with a handful of players tells you little.
Clearly labelled simulated forecastersAbout 200 fill the market, marked as simulated on every screen. Real ones join in the final round.
One quarter is hard to callIn replay the market beats a coin flip only just: Brier 0.242 against 0.25.
The market re-weights, never replacesExpiry dates start from public exposure and adoption data. The three futures stay on screen, labelled as estimates.
The hardest risk: official data is rare. So we replay sixty-six real past releases, and we label our simulated forecasters everywhere. And we are honest: in replay the market only just beats a coin flip, so it re-weights the futures and never replaces the data.
